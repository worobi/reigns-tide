Family story

The day everything changed

My name is Kerrin Bowerson, and I am a 32-year-old single mother raising four children on my own. Every day, I do everything I can to provide a stable, loving life for my kids: my 12-year-old son Vincent, my 10-year-old daughter Harley, and my 3-year-old identical twin girls, Raven and Reign.

Up until recently, I was working full-time as a cook at The Vines Hospital in Ocala, Florida, while working toward becoming a Mental Health Technician. My twins attended a Christian daycare they absolutely loved, surrounded by teachers who have cared for them since they were babies, while my older two children attended public school.

Our lives changed suddenly and forever on March 24, 2026.

While I was at work, I missed several urgent calls from my twins' daycare. When I finally saw the messages, I learned that Reign's diaper was filled with blood. I immediately left work, picked her up, and rushed her to the emergency room. After hours of waiting and testing, doctors discovered a large tumor on her right kidney.

Later that night, I drove Reign myself to UF Health Shands, a hospital an hour and a half away, so she could receive the care she needed. The next day, after further scans, doctors confirmed the devastating news: Reign had cancer.

She was diagnosed with Wilms Tumor, a rare kidney cancer, and her condition was advanced. The cancer had spread from her kidney to both of her lungs.

Hearing the words "your child has cancer" is something no parent is ever prepared for. In that moment, my world completely shattered. I was overwhelmed, terrified, and alone, trying to process everything while staying strong for my baby.

Reign has always been my little fighter. Even before she was born, doctors found a mass on her lung. She was born prematurely at 35 weeks and spent time in the NICU. At just 8 months old, she had surgery to remove part of her lung, and later endured another surgery for a serious infection. In her short three years of life, she already faced more than most.

And now, she is resting after facing the biggest battle of all.

During treatment, Reign underwent weekly chemotherapy treatments that required long trips to UF Health Shands. Because of her condition, she could no longer attend daycare, and I had to stop working to care for her full-time.

I did everything I could to stay strong, not just for Reign, but for all four of my children.
